The big questions

Where computing gets human.

A suggested thematic guide to the module. Use these topics to connect technical choices with their wider consequences.

01 / PRIVACY

Who controls our data?

Explore consent, personal information, surveillance, and the tension between useful services and individual privacy.

02 / INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

Who owns what we create?

Discuss software licensing, attribution, copyright, open source, and the responsible reuse of digital work.

03 / CYBERSECURITY

What does responsible access mean?

Examine authorization, security research, vulnerability disclosure, and the effects of misuse on people and institutions.

04 / ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Can an algorithm be fair?

Investigate bias, transparency, accountability, and the role of human judgment in automated decisions.

05 / PROFESSIONAL CONDUCT

What do professionals owe society?

Consider competence, honesty, confidentiality, conflicts of interest, and speaking up when a system may cause harm.

06 / DIGITAL SOCIETY

Who benefits—and who is excluded?

Reflect on accessibility, digital inequality, online expression, environmental impact, and inclusive design.

Learning with purpose

Move from an opinion
to a reasoned position.

Suggested learning goals for thoughtful discussion, evidence-based argument, and responsible computing practice.

  • ↗Identify the stakeholders, interests, and potential harms in a computing dilemma.
  • ↗Distinguish legal obligations from ethical responsibilities.
  • ↗Compare decisions using consequences, duties, rights, and fairness.
  • ↗Support an argument with credible sources and acknowledge uncertainty.
  • ↗Recommend practical safeguards and explain the trade-offs.

Prepare · Discuss · Reflect

Make the seminar count.

01
Before the session

Read the assigned material. Bring one relevant example, one credible source, and one question you cannot yet answer.

02
During the discussion

Map stakeholders, test assumptions, and respectfully challenge arguments. Separate what is known from what is inferred.

03
After the session

Write a short reflection: your position, its strongest objection, and what evidence would change your mind.

Legal research checklist

Identify the relevant jurisdiction, locate the primary legal text, check its effective date and amendments, and explain how it applies to the facts. Follow your lecturer’s assigned sources.

A framework for your argument

State the dilemma → identify stakeholders → establish facts → consider applicable rules → compare ethical options → recommend an action → acknowledge limitations.
